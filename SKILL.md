# Text-First Exegesis

Use this evidence hierarchy:

**textual facts > literary facts > historical facts > existing exegetical viewpoints > theology**

- **Textual facts**: recoverable wording and variants.
- **Literary facts**: the target literary work as a whole: language, syntax, narrative/discourse, rhetoric, structure, participants, repeated constructions, lexical networks.
- **Historical facts**: wider language, culture, society, archaeology, intertexts, other texts/books, and compositional history. Cross-book evidence belongs here.
- **Existing exegetical viewpoints**: scholarly interpretations, arguments, and research distribution. Scholarly authority is not evidence.
- **Theology**: canonical, biblical-theological, systematic, doctrinal, reception, ecclesial, and pastoral judgments.

The hierarchy is **directional and non-compensatory**. Each higher layer constrains the admissible space for lower layers. A lower layer may refine, contextualize, or rank readings that remain compatible with the frozen higher layer; it may not outweigh or overturn that layer by accumulation. If lower-layer evidence appears to overturn a higher-layer result, identify the specific missed variable or error, reopen that higher layer, and rerun it using only evidence admissible to that layer. Do not count lower-layer evidence as higher-layer evidence.

For intertexts specifically: recognizing an echo/allusion is a **historical fact**. The source text's referent, chronology, ontology, or function does **not** transfer automatically into the target work. Such transfer must be independently established by the target work's own literary evidence.

Do not let theology or canonical status control historical exegesis.

Treat the **entire target book as both the minimum and maximum synchronic literary object**. A local passage is an analytical window into the work, not an autonomous interpretive world. If historical evidence establishes a genuinely separate literary stage with its own historical readers, analyze that stage as its own whole literary object.

## Dual-track protocol

Always produce both tracks for substantive exegesis. Keep them independent.

### A. Independent reconstruction

Freeze Track A **before consulting modern exegetical scholarship or Track B**.

1. **Text** — Establish wording and relevant variants without using textual criticism to decide interpretation.
2. **Literature** — Map the whole book before letting the immediate context control the issue. State `FORM/SYNTAX`, `MINIMAL BOOK-INTERNAL SENSE`, and every unresolved referent, domain, event type, temporal frame, participant relation, or neighboring ambiguity on which the interpretation depends.
3. **Book-internal testing** — For each controlling variable, inspect the closest comparable uses across the whole book before broad thematic proof texts. Do not hide disputed conclusions inside translations or observations; unresolved variables may not prove one another.
4. **Rivals** — When a real dispute exists, construct at least two strong book-internal models. Rank them only by observations that actually distinguish them; shared predictions cannot decide the dispute.
5. **History** — After a literary freeze, use wider language, intertexts, other books, culture, society, archaeology, and composition only to refine or rank models still compatible with that freeze. Historical evidence may expose a missed literary variable, but then reopen and rerun the literary layer with target-book evidence alone; do not let history directly overturn the literary freeze.
6. **Freeze A** — State the independent historical-exegetical conclusion, serious alternatives, and unresolved questions. Treat A as an exploratory AI reconstruction, not frontier authority. For a substantive disputed interpretation, do **not** label A's overall conclusion high-confidence; use provisional/low/moderate confidence even when some component facts are secure.

Track A must not use named scholars, commentary conclusions, remembered consensus, or Track B as premises. A is intentionally vulnerable to later criticism; that is part of the design.

### B. Current-frontier reconstruction

Begin only after A is frozen. Start the research afresh; **A's ranking and evidence selection are not evidence for B**. Before any frontier search, read `references/frontier-search.md` and follow its discovery protocol.

#### 1. Discover candidates before qualifying them

Do **high-recall discovery first** and author/venue qualification second. Do not use author provenance, commentary-series reputation, or an early leading interpretation to narrow the candidate search.

The mandatory discovery sweep includes: exact passage, whole literary unit, **composition/source/redaction/original-form blind-spot searches**, controlling variables, adversarial challenge to the current leader, recent field-level synthesis hubs, and a **platform matrix**. When relevant, Cambridge and Oxford must be searched separately, followed by at least two additional high-level scholarly ecosystems. Build the discovery ledger and meet its recall floor **before** provenance or method filtering. Use the detailed query families and stop rule in `references/frontier-search.md`.

Mine recent field-level handbooks/companions/state-of-research volumes as **researcher maps**: when two suitable frontier-eligible hubs exist, use at least two. Treat **every editor as a mandatory seed** and also seed the relevant contributors into passage/unit-specific searches, even when those scholars' important titles do not contain the target lemma. Search each seed by exact passage, named unit, and unit + composition/interpretation before pruning. This seeding is for recall only; it gives no exegetical authority and does not itself establish frontier status.

If backward tracing lands before the 15-year cutoff, use it only to identify lineage, then trace forward again to a frontier-eligible recent carrier before counting the proposal. **Finding a scholar, title, abstract, review, or metadata is not yet finding the proposal.**

#### 2. Apply a rolling 15-year frontier gate

For Track B, **secondary scholarship is frontier-eligible only if published within the current year minus 15 years, inclusive**. In 2026, use 2011-present scholarship. This cutoff applies to modern secondary scholarship, not to primary texts, manuscripts, critical editions, lexical data, archaeology, or other first-order evidence.

A pre-cutoff scholarly work has **no independent frontier weight**, however famous or authoritative. If an older proposal remains live, represent it through frontier-eligible recent scholarship that adopts, develops, revises, or criticizes it. Older scholarship may be opened only as historical background when an eligible recent work explicitly requires it; do not use it to establish current distribution, carry the target conclusion, or rescue a proposal absent from recent literature.

#### 3. Recover each major proposal before counting it as mapped

For every directly relevant major work or scholar discovered, recover enough of the actual argument to state: **thesis, target passage/unit, primary evidence, and decisive inference**. If only a review, abstract, contents page, or citation is available, keep searching **frontier-eligible** publications, accessible chapters/articles, lectures, repositories, substantive recent reviews, and the recent citation network. A recent technical commentary should trigger a search for the author's other directly relevant work **inside the 15-year window** when that may expose the underlying argument.

If the position still cannot be recovered from eligible recent material, mark it **position not recovered**. Do not reconstruct it from pre-cutoff scholarship, summarize it from guesswork, count it as covered, or use it to establish the frontier.

#### 4. Audit author provenance before assigning frontier status

Do not infer frontier status from a commentary series, journal, or publisher label alone. For every major directly relevant candidate author, trace the author's scholarly background using current institutional profiles, CVs, publisher pages, and bibliography metadata. This provenance audit is **for discovery and coverage only; it is never exegetical evidence**.

Use two admission paths:

- **Established-frontier author path** — require a sustained, field-relevant record of several substantial research books/monographs with leading university presses such as Cambridge, Oxford, Yale, Harvard, Edinburgh, or a genuinely comparable academic press. A single chapter, edited volume contribution, textbook, or one prestigious publication is not enough.
- **Emerging-frontier proposal path** — a proposal may still count as a serious live development when its author lacks that established record, but only if frontier-eligible recent research shows substantial uptake: repeated adoption, revision, or criticism by established-frontier scholars or by multiple high-level recent treatments. In this case label the **proposal** emerging/live; do not upgrade the author to established-frontier status.

Treat commentary series only as **discovery priors**:
- **Hermeneia, EKK, IECOT** — strong positive prior for where to look first; still audit the author and the actual argument.
- **Anchor Yale Bible (AYB)** — positive prior, but author-level verification remains mandatory.
- **ICC** — neutral/variable prior; never infer frontier status from the series name.
- **Other commentary series** — no frontier status from series reputation alone.

Do not force a publisher-before-journal sequence. Search by **direct relevance and research network**, then use author provenance and current uptake to decide which candidates deserve full recovery. A directly relevant recent article can outrank an adjacent prestigious book for coverage purposes.

#### 5. Assess every serious proposal on independent fields

For each serious proposal record:
- **Recency** — frontier-eligible under the rolling 15-year gate or not.
- **Author provenance** — established-frontier, not established, or not recovered.
- **Current uptake** — repeatedly adopted/revised/criticized in frontier-eligible research, limited uptake, or unclear.
- **Direct relevance** — directly addresses the disputed expression/unit/controlling argument, partly relevant, or adjacent/background.
- **Method compatibility** — compatible, partly compatible, or incompatible with **textual → literary → historical → exegetical → theological**.

Derive **frontier status** from recency, provenance, uptake, and direct relevance. Treat **direct relevance as a gate** for target-level claims. Treat **method compatibility as an admissibility/audit gate, not a score for ranking otherwise compatible proposals**. If a proposal is compatible, do not award or subtract evidential weight because of the label. Author provenance and current uptake determine what must be taken seriously and recovered; they never prove the interpretation correct.

#### 6. Steelman before rebuttal

For every major **method-compatible** frontier proposal, do not stop after recovering its thesis. Recover its strongest target-book evidence, decisive variable/construction, treatment of the most obvious counterevidence, and frontier-eligible direct adoption/criticism. If the proposal's own response to an apparent objection has not been recovered, that objection is **not yet a decisive rebuttal**.

Apply an **epistemic rebuttal burden**. Track B may not rank a method-compatible frontier proposal below another merely because A or the model can produce an alternative reading, a few apparently contrary passages, or a more familiar interpretation. To defeat such a proposal, require either (a) frontier-eligible, method-compatible scholarship that directly rebuts its decisive primary-evidence argument, or (b) a clear first-three-layer error that is independently checkable without presupposing the disputed interpretation, plus verification that the proposal or its recent defenders do not already answer it. Otherwise report the dispute as unresolved under this method rather than forcing an AI verdict.

This asymmetry is about **depth of recovered argument and the model's limited independent research capacity**, not scholarly authority. Scholar names, venues, and prestige still have zero exegetical force.

#### 7. Decompose and re-adjudicate every serious proposal

Before adopting or rejecting a frontier proposal, split its argument into:

```text
TEXTUAL EVIDENCE
LITERARY EVIDENCE
HISTORICAL EVIDENCE
EXEGETICAL INFERENCES
THEOLOGICAL / CANONICAL INFERENCES
```

Then rerun the hierarchy in order:
- freeze textual facts;
- freeze the target work's literary evidence without cross-book/intertextual control;
- introduce historical evidence only inside the possibility space left by that literary freeze;
- use existing exegetical proposals only as hypotheses/arguments to test, never as evidence by authority;
- keep theology/canon downstream.

Apply a **non-reversal rule**: historical/intertextual evidence cannot directly defeat a literary result merely because an external source has a strong verbal echo, older chronology, familiar referent, or established theological function. An intertext may establish dependence or deliberate evocation while the target work redeploys the source language for a different referent or function. If historical evidence reveals a serious missed literary variable, reopen the literary layer and rerun it using target-book evidence alone before changing the literary ranking.

For each serious proposal also ask whether it:
- lets whole-target-book literary evidence control the literary layer;
- keeps cross-book/intertextual evidence in the historical layer rather than making it a canonical or source-text key;
- distinguishes textual/literary/historical facts from interpretive conclusions;
- prevents theology, canon, or a larger biblical-theological scheme from flowing backward into earlier layers.

If a frontier view violates this direction, **still report its real scholarly status**, identify the backflow/category error, strip away the later-layer premise, and retest whatever earlier-layer case remains. Method incompatibility lowers adoptability under this method; it does not erase the view from the frontier map.

#### 8. Establish coverage before claiming the frontier

Before freezing B, verify all of the following:
- every mandatory discovery family in `references/frontier-search.md` was actually attempted, including the composition blind-spot search and the platform matrix;
- the discovery-ledger recall floor was met or a concrete access/field-size limitation was recorded;
- at least two field-hub seeding passes were completed when two suitable frontier-eligible hubs exist, with every editor actually searched as a researcher seed;
- the strongest serious alternatives discovered by adversarial searching are represented;
- each major directly relevant proposal has been recovered as an argument, not just a citation;
- each major method-compatible frontier proposal has been steelmanned far enough to recover its strongest target-book case and its response to obvious counterevidence, or the missing response is explicitly reported;
- the 15-year secondary-scholarship gate has been enforced;
- author provenance was audited for major candidates, and series reputation was used only as a discovery prior;
- at least one frontier-eligible high-level synthesis or technical treatment has been used to trace the debate;
- older proposals appear only when carried, developed, revised, or contested by frontier-eligible recent scholarship.

Do not infer consensus or say the field is concentrated merely because early results agree. If adversarial searching finds no serious rival, say so only after the coverage gate is complete.

#### 9. Freeze B

State the frontier map, the strongest competing views, their multi-field assessments, the primary evidence that actually drives each view, and the literature distribution when reasonably knowable. Give a substantive ranking **under this method** only when the rebuttal burden above is met. Otherwise state that the method-compatible frontier remains unresolved rather than manufacturing a winner from the model's independent reading.

If B finds a proposal A missed, record the difference. **Never revise A retroactively.**

## Output

1. **A — Independent reconstruction**: Textual facts → Literary facts → Historical facts → Independent conclusion.
2. **B — Current-frontier reconstruction**: Frontier map (**recency + author provenance + current uptake + direct relevance + method compatibility + derived status**) → steelman of major method-compatible proposals → proposal decomposition by evidence layer → ordered re-adjudication → frontier conclusion or explicit unresolved judgment when the rebuttal burden is unmet.
3. **Comparison and critique**: after both freezes, state convergence and divergence, then let B **adversarially audit A**: identify frontier models A missed, evidence A omitted or misweighted, loaded assumptions, and any overconfidence. Keep A frozen rather than rewriting it. Critique A at the same layer: B may overturn A's literary ranking only with a stronger/more complete **target-book literary** case recovered through scholarship, not with historical/intertextual evidence alone. Historical evidence may correct A's historical layer or trigger a literary rerun, but cannot substitute for that rerun. When A conflicts with a method-compatible frontier argument that has been steelmanned and not adequately rebutted, downgrade A's contrary confidence; do **not** let A's shallower independent reading defeat that frontier argument. Scholar prestige is never the reason—the recovered argument is.
4. **Theology / canon / reception / pastoral application**: only when requested, clearly downstream from both tracks.

For brief questions, keep both tracks compact rather than omitting one.

Read `references/frontier-search.md` only when beginning Track B research. Do not read `references/methodology.md` during normal exegesis; use it only to explain, audit, or revise this method. Never read `references/regression-tests.md` during live exegesis.
