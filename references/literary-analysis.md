# Literary Analysis Protocol

Load before Layer 2 in both A and B1.

## 1. Internal order

Layer 2 is directional and non-compensatory:

**genre constraints > whole-book macrostructure > episode/unit relation > local discourse/form > syntax/lexeme**

Run the analysis in that order. Lower-level evidence may refine a relation left open above it; it may not overturn a settled higher-level judgment merely by accumulation.

## 2. Whole-book before local sequence

For structure-sensitive questions, establish:

- operative genre and its conventions;
- major whole-book units/cycles/scenes and framing devices;
- repeated or parallel endpoints;
- temporal horizons and resets;
- irreversible state changes;
- the target unit's relation to adjacent and parallel units.

Classify the unit relation as appropriate: chronological advance, recapitulation/re-presentation, parallelism, zoom/explanation, flashback, prolepsis, thematic juxtaposition, frame return, or unresolved.

## 3. Keep orders distinct

Distinguish:

- text/discourse order;
- report/vision/speech order;
- chronology internal to one unit;
- the temporal horizon at a unit's opening and endpoint;
- represented event order;
- logical/explanatory order.

Page order and transition formulas establish discourse succession. They establish event succession only when same-object genre and macrostructure supply positive warrant.

A later unit's endpoint may presuppose an earlier terminal state without proving that the later unit **began** after the earlier unit ended. Test the later unit's opening horizon independently. Reappearance of actors, conflicts, or world conditions apparently exhausted by an earlier terminal scene is positive evidence for reset, overlap, or re-presentation and must be explained before claiming linear progression.

## 4. Chronology burden

A chronology-dependent model must explain the whole-book map, not only local adjacency. Positive warrant can include explicit temporal anchoring, unbroken continuity of an irreversible story-world state, repeated book-level use of comparable transitions as temporal progression, or causal dependence that requires the earlier represented event to have occurred first.

In visionary, symbolic, poetic, prophetic, or heavily rhetorical discourse, surface sequence has no independent power to establish represented chronology. In narrative it has more prima facie force, while flashback, summary, prolepsis, topical arrangement, and embedded discourse still require classification.

If a model's load-bearing move is effectively `later in discourse/report -> later in represented event time` without adequate same-object structural warrant, mark **`LAYER 2 SEQUENCE-CONFLATION FAILURE`**.

## 5. Lexical restraint

Keep lexical sense separate from contextual referent, discourse function, event type, ontology, and chronology. Repeated wording or the same lexeme can support semantic continuity; it does not by itself prove that two passages depict the same kind of event in the same temporal relation.

## 6. Rival test

Represent serious rivals as:

`GENRE -> MACROSTRUCTURE -> UNIT RELATION -> LOCAL MARKERS -> TARGET CLAIM`

Locate the first substantive divergence and adjudicate there with evidence from that level or higher. Do not allow stronger syntax, intertext, theology, tradition, or popularity to compensate for a failed higher literary relation.

## 7. Required output for structure/chronology disputes

Show compactly:

```text
GENRE:
WHOLE-BOOK MACROSTRUCTURE:
UNIT RELATION:
CHRONOLOGY WARRANT / RESET TEST:
LOCAL DISCOURSE & SYNTAX:
MINIMAL OBJECT-INTERNAL SENSE:
MODEL CONSEQUENCE:
```

Use the locked object's evidence. Do not preassign a book-, passage-, or doctrine-specific result.
