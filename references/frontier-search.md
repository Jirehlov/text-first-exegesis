# Frontier Discovery Protocol

Load this file only for Track B. Its purpose is **candidate recall**, not exegetical adjudication.

## Principle

Do not begin by deciding which authors or series are frontier. First build a high-recall candidate pool; only then apply recency, author provenance, current uptake, direct relevance, and method compatibility.

Author provenance is a **qualification gate after discovery**, never a search filter before discovery.

## Mandatory discovery sweep

Before Track B may claim coverage, execute at least one genuinely distinct search in **each** family below. Batch queries when the search tool permits, but do not replace one family with cosmetic variants of another.

1. **Exact passage** — exact passage/range + interpretation/debate/reassessment.
2. **Whole literary unit** — named unit/section + interpretation/debate/new reading.
3. **Composition blind-spot search** — named unit/section + composition/source/redaction/original form/literary history. This is mandatory because important proposals often have titles that do not contain the target lemma.
4. **Control-variable search** — each decisive clause, construction, discourse frame, referent, or event type + interpretation.
5. **Adversarial search** — the provisional leading model + alternative/reassessment/critique/revision/competing reading. Generate this from the current leader; do not presuppose a known rival.
6. **Field-hub search** — recent handbook/companion/state-of-research volume for the target book or field from leading university-press research ecosystems.
7. **Platform-matrix sweep** — run the whole-unit and composition query separately on **Cambridge Core/Cambridge University Press** and **Oxford Academic/Oxford University Press** whenever those platforms cover the field. Then repeat on at least two additional high-level ecosystems appropriate to the field, such as Yale, Harvard, Edinburgh, Mohr Siebeck, Brill, Bloomsbury/T&T Clark, De Gruyter, or comparable specialist academic platforms. A broad web search is not a substitute. Do not merge this into one generic query: the Cambridge and Oxford passes are distinct mandatory searches when relevant.

If a family yields mostly popular/confessional material, reformulate it with the exact passage/unit and a scholarly platform/domain rather than treating that family as completed.


## Discovery ledger and recall floor

Before qualifying or pruning candidates, build an internal **DISCOVERY LEDGER**. Record the actual query family/platform and every plausible scholar/work it returns. Do not show the ledger unless useful, but do not proceed without it.

For a substantive passage/unit dispute, do not treat discovery as complete until all of the following are true:
- the ledger contains candidates from at least **four distinct search families**;
- Cambridge and Oxford were searched separately when they host relevant field research;
- at least **two frontier-eligible field hubs** were mined when two suitable hubs exist;
- every hub editor was searched by `author + exact passage`, `author + named unit`, and `author + unit + composition/interpretation`;
- at least three relevant hub contributors per hub were searched the same way when available;
- at least **eight unique candidate scholars/works** were harvested before provenance/method filtering, unless the field is demonstrably smaller;
- at least two genuinely different interpretive clusters have been sought. If only one cluster appears, run another adversarial and composition search rather than concluding consensus.

These are **recall floors**, not frontier-status rules. Candidates may later be rejected for recency, relevance, provenance, uptake, or method.

## Frontier-hub seeding

Locate **at least two** frontier-eligible field-level hubs when two suitable hubs exist: handbooks, companions, state-of-research volumes, or comparable research syntheses from leading university presses. If only one suitable recent hub exists, use one and record the limitation. These are **maps of researchers**, not authorities for the interpretation.

From each hub:
- always seed **every editor** for follow-up searches, even when their own chapter titles do not mention the target passage; an editor is a mandatory researcher seed, not an optional suggestion;
- seed contributors whose chapters concern the target book's literary sources, composition, history, language, central themes, or the target unit;
- search each seed author by `author + exact passage`, `author + named unit`, and when relevant `author + unit + composition/interpretation`;
- then trace the author's frontier-eligible directly relevant publications and recent citation network.

Do not require a seed author to pass the established-frontier provenance gate before searching them. The hub is used to avoid title/keyword blind spots; provenance is assessed only after a directly relevant proposal is found.

## Proposal recovery

A person or title counts as a mapped proposal only after recovering:
- thesis;
- target passage/unit;
- primary evidence;
- decisive inference.

For every major method-compatible proposal that may affect the final judgment, continue to **steelman depth**:
- strongest target-book literary evidence;
- decisive variable/construction or inferential hinge;
- treatment of the most obvious apparent counterevidence;
- frontier-eligible direct adoption, revision, or criticism.

If only metadata, a review, abstract, or publisher description is visible, continue through frontier-eligible articles/chapters, repositories, lectures, substantive reviews, and forward/backward citations. If the author's response to an apparent objection cannot be recovered, record that gap; do not convert the unrecovered objection into a decisive rebuttal.

## Citation-network pass

For each directly relevant high-level work:
- inspect references or bibliographic neighbors for rival proposals;
- trace important pre-cutoff ancestors only as lineage, then trace forward to a frontier-eligible recent carrier;
- search the author's later work to see whether the proposal was retained, revised, or abandoned;
- search frontier-eligible works that cite or directly discuss the proposal when available;
- for each major method-compatible proposal, explicitly search for direct critique/reassessment and for the author's or defenders' response to the strongest apparent objection before ranking it below a rival.

## Stop rule

Do not freeze B until:
- all seven search families were actually attempted;
- the discovery-ledger recall floor was met, or an explicit field-size/access limitation was recorded;
- at least two field-hub seeding passes were completed when two suitable hubs exist;
- Cambridge and Oxford platform sweeps were completed separately when relevant, plus at least two additional high-level platform sweeps;
- major directly relevant proposals were recovered as arguments rather than names;
- major method-compatible proposals that affect the conclusion were recovered to steelman depth, including their handling of obvious counterevidence when recoverable;
- new searches and citation tracing are no longer producing serious live models or direct rebuttals that materially change the dispute.

Do not equate failure of one query with absence of a proposal. Change the search level: lemma -> passage -> unit -> composition -> researcher network.
